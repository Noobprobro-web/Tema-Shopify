## Cero: la marca ficticia y una advertencia legal

**Nombre de la casa: LINEA NOVE.** Forma corta: **Nove**.

Es un nombre inventado por mí para esta demo. Italiano, "línea nueve", plausible para una casa de prêt-à-porter y sin parecido con ninguna casa existente que yo conozca. Dicho eso, **antes de publicar la demo hay que hacer una búsqueda de marca registrada**, porque Shopify exige derechos sobre todos los assets de la demo y un nombre que colisione con una marca real es exactamente el tipo de cosa que frena una submission. Si aparece un conflicto, el nombre se cambia en una pasada de buscar y reemplazar: no está horneado en ninguna parte estructural.

Requisito relacionado que conviene tener a la vista: Shopify pide **texto auténtico, prohibido Lorem Ipsum**, en todas las páginas de la demo. Todo lo que sigue está escrito para cumplir eso. Ninguna línea es relleno.

## Uno: posicionamiento, para que el copy tenga una sola voz

Nove es una casa pequeña de prêt-à-porter femenino. Produce en series cortas, dos colecciones al año más un núcleo permanente que no cambia. Materiales naturales y trazables, corte arquitectónico, paleta reducida a tinta, hueso, arcilla y un verde oliva apagado.

El registro de la voz, que es lo que hay que sostener en las 24 descripciones y en las páginas:

**Sí:** la materia, el proceso, la medida, la intención de uso. Frases cortas. Sustantivos concretos. Dejar que el objeto hable.

**No:** superlativos ("exquisito", "lujoso", "atemporal"), lenguaje de campaña vacío ("una celebración de la feminidad"), y sobre todo no explicar el sentimiento que el cliente debería tener. Una casa de este nivel describe y se calla.

Prueba rápida para cualquier línea nueva: si la frase funcionaría igual para un bolso o para un perfume, es vaga. Si solo funciona para esta prenda, sirve.

### `/pages/care-and-repairs`

- Small label: `Care and repairs`
- Introduction: `We repair anything we made, free, for as long as we hold the pattern. Most repairs take two weeks.`

> **Wool and cashmere.** Air the garment between wearings and it will need cleaning far less often than you think. When it does, dry clean sparingly: every cycle costs the fibre a little. Store folded, never on a hanger, and never in plastic.
>
> **Alpaca.** Pills in the first month. That is the shorter fibres working themselves out and it stops. Use a comb, not a razor.
>
> **Silk.** Hand wash cold with a pH-neutral soap, roll in a towel, dry flat away from sun. Iron on the reverse while slightly damp.
>
> **Leather curtido al vegetal.** Nothing, for the first year. It will darken unevenly and that is the point. After that, a neutral cream twice a year.
>
> **Repairs.** Write to repairs@lineanove.com with a photo and your order number. We will tell you within two business days whether we can fix it, what it will take and when you will have it back. If we cannot fix it, we will say so.

## Siete: tres artículos de journal

Shopify quiere el blog poblado. Tres artículos, con excerpt y texto completo suficiente para que la plantilla se vea real.

### 1. `Why we stopped lining coats`

**Excerpt:** `Removing the lining made the Serra Coat lighter, warmer and repairable. It also made it considerably harder to sew.`

**Tags:** `Process, Materials`

> A lining is the part of a coat that fails first. It is usually cupro or viscose, it is sewn in last, and when it splits under the arm the coat becomes a repair job that costs more than the lining is worth. Most people stop wearing the coat instead.
>
> So the Serra has no lining. The wool is double-faced, which means it is two cloths woven and bonded as one, with a finished face on both sides. There is no inside. The seams are bound by hand in a matching tape and the hem is closed with a blind stitch that takes one of our machinists about forty minutes.
>
> This is worse for us in every commercial sense. The cloth costs more than twice a single-faced wool. It cannot be eased or steamed into shape the way a lined coat can, which means the pattern has to be right before the first cut. And there is no lining to hide anything, so every seam is visible from the inside and has to be clean.
>
> What we get is a coat that weighs about four hundred grams less, is warmer because there is no slippery layer between you and the wool, and can be taken apart and re-bound in fifteen years.
>
> The first run of forty took us three attempts to get right. The pattern we use now is the third one.

### 2. `Forty pieces, and why not four hundred`

**Excerpt:** `A short explanation of what a series is, why they sell out, and why we will not remake them.`

**Tags:** `Process`

> When we say a piece is cut in a series of forty, we mean that forty exist and that there will not be more.
>
> The reason is not scarcity as a sales tactic. It is that our mills weave to order and the minimum they will make for a house our size is roughly enough cloth for forty garments. Ordering more would mean committing to cloth we might not sell, in a colour that might be wrong, before anyone has seen the piece. Houses with warehouses can absorb that. We cannot.
>
> So we order once, cut once, and when it is gone it is gone. If a piece sells out in a week that tells us something and it goes into the permanent collection next year, in the same cloth, in the same cut. If it takes six months to sell, that tells us something too.
>
> What we will not do is remake a sold-out piece in a cheaper fabric and give it the same name. That happens often enough in this industry that it is worth saying plainly.

### 3. `How to buy one coat instead of three`

**Excerpt:** `An honest guide to spending more once, including the cases where you should not.`

**Tags:** `Notes`

> We sell coats, so read this with that in mind.
>
> Buying one expensive coat instead of three cheap ones works when three things are true. The cut suits your actual shape rather than the shape you intend to have. The colour goes with at least half of what you already own. And you can repair it, which means asking before you buy whether the maker will.
>
> When those three are not true, the expensive coat becomes an expensive mistake that you keep out of guilt. We have made that coat for people and we would rather not.
>
> The practical test: take the garment you have worn most in the last two years. Not your favourite, the one you actually wore. Look at its length, its colour and how it closes. Buy in that direction. Ambition is a bad pattern cutter.
>
> And measure. Every product page here lists the garment's own measurements. Compare them to something in your wardrobe with a tape measure on a flat surface. It takes four minutes and it prevents most returns, including ours.

## Nota sobre reutilización para Studio y Archive

El posicionamiento, el catálogo y la voz de Nove son de **Couture**. Studio y Archive necesitan sus propias casas ficticias, sus propios catálogos y su propia voz, porque el requisito de Shopify es que cada demo corresponda a su industria y a su tamaño de catálogo. Reciclar el mismo catálogo con otra tipografía es exactamente lo que un revisor detecta.

Cuando quieran arranco con las otras dos. Studio pide objetos y accesorios de diseño con catálogo de 40 a 60 productos y voz mucho más seca. Archive pide denim, cuero o textil heritage con voz de catálogo y numeral de índice.
